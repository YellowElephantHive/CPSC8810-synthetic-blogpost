# Hybrid Synthetic Data Augmentation Analysis

This post studies how synthetic images—produced by modern generative models—affect the performance of image classifiers when mixed with real training data. As diffusion-based systems become increasingly capable, it is natural to ask a forward-looking question: *How much synthetic data can we safely use, and in what regimes does it actually help?*  
Using CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, we evaluate performance across different real–synthetic ratios and examine class-level effects, distribution gaps, and model-dependent behaviors.

---

## Problem Statement

Deep-learning classifiers rely on large, diverse datasets. In practical applications such as robotics or industrial inspection, collecting large real datasets is expensive. Synthetic data generated by diffusion models offers a scalable alternative.  
However, two questions remain open:

1. **How does the mixing ratio of real and synthetic data affect accuracy?**  
2. **Does the realism or consistency of synthetic data matter across different datasets and models?**

This project quantifies how performance shifts as synthetic data increasingly replaces—or supplements—real images, under controlled experimental conditions.

---

## Related Work

- *Synthetic Data from Diffusion Models Improves ImageNet Classification*, Azizi et al., 2023  
- *Image Retrieval Outperforms Diffusion Models on Data Augmentation*, Burg et al., 2023  
- *Advances in Diffusion Models for Image Data Augmentation*, Alimisis et al., 2025  
- *Survey on Data Augmentation in the Large Model Era*, Zhou et al., 2024  

These works show that synthetic images can match or exceed real data in some settings, but also highlight domain shift risks. Our experiments extend these ideas to explicit ratio-controlled settings for CIFAR-10/100.

---

## Approach

### Data Generation

We produce synthetic samples using:

- **Stable Diffusion** (CIFAR-100 experiments)  
- **FLUX-schnell** (CIFAR-10 experiments)

Synthetic images are generated class-conditionally using structured prompts. For each class, real and synthetic images are sampled using fixed random seeds to ensure consistent dataset composition across all ratios.

### Hybrid Dataset Construction

For CIFAR-100

- Mix ratios ∈ {100:0, 80:20, 60:40, 50:50, 40:60, 20:80, 0:100}

For CIFAR-10

- Mix ratios ∈ {100:0, 90:10, ..., 0:100}

We also examine a separate **data-addition regime**, where synthetic images are added on top of all real samples (e.g., 100% real + f·synthetic).

### Model Training

All classifiers are trained under identical conditions:

- CIFAR-100: ResNet-18  
- CIFAR-10: ResNet-18 and a small CNN  
- Same augmentation, optimizer, batch size, and training epochs  
- Evaluation always conducted on the real test set  
- Metrics: mean, max, and min accuracy across runs; per-class recall (CIFAR-100)

## Prompt Engineering

To generate class-aligned synthetic images, we use:

- **Class-aware prompts** tuned to CIFAR-100 / CIFAR-10 categories  
- **Prompt pools** to introduce controlled randomness  
- Constraints to maintain dataset consistency:
  - single object  
  - centered composition  
  - simple background  
  - no multiple instances  
- **Negative prompts** to remove text, artifacts, logos, or unnatural textures  

These constraints reduce inter-class ambiguity and prevent common diffusion-model failure modes.

# CIFAR-100 Experiment

## Setting

- **Generator**: Stable Diffusion  
- **Generation resolution**: 512×512 (later downsampled to CIFAR resolution)  
- **Sampling step**: 15  
- **Real dataset**: 100 classes × 500 real images each  
- **Synthetic dataset**: generated 1:1 per class  
- **Mixing**: Real:Synthetic in specified ratios  
- **Sampling rule**: fixed seed; per-class uniform sampling of real/synthetic images to match ratios

## Experiment Results (Stable Diffusion)

### Best Validation Accuracy vs Mix Ratio

![](slide11_img1.png)

The bar chart shows a clear monotonic degradation as synthetic content increases. 80:20 shows slight improvement over 100% real, but beyond 60% synthetic the model’s performance drops rapidly. A purely synthetic dataset collapses almost entirely.

---

### Per-Class Recall Heatmap

![](slide12_img1.png)

This heatmap reveals how individual classes respond to synthetic mixing:

- **Weak classes** (low recall in the real-only setting) often improve when moderate synthetic data is introduced. Synthetic images help compensate for under-represented or visually ambiguous classes.
- **Strong classes** (high real-recall baseline) degrade as synthetic proportion increases. The synthetic distribution diverges from the real visual appearance, causing systematic errors.
- **≥80% synthetic** strongly harms nearly all classes.

---

## CIFAR-100 Conclusion

- Synthetic data helps **only when added in moderation**.  
- Gains occur primarily in **low-recall classes**, consistent with synthetic data acting as targeted augmentation.  
- High-recall classes degrade due to domain mismatch and over-regularization.  
- Performance collapses under extreme synthetic ratios—realism gaps become dominant.

# CIFAR-10 Experiment

## Example Visualization

![](slide15_img1.png)

CIFAR-10 categories are simpler and visually cleaner than CIFAR-100. Synthetic generation at 128×128 resolution matches the dataset’s style more closely, resulting in smaller domain shift.

---

## Setting

- **Generator**: FLUX-schnell  
- **Resolution**: 128×128  
- **Sampling step**: 4  
- **Real dataset**: 5000 images per class  
- **Models**: ResNet-18 and a simple CNN  
- **Mix ratios**: 100:0 → 0:100  
- **Two regimes**:
  1. **Mixing** (fixed total size)  
  2. **Adding synthetic** (increasing total size)

---

## Experiment: CNN Mixing

![](slide17_img1.png)

Mean accuracy remains nearly flat across mix ratios. The model does not degrade when synthetic content increases, suggesting synthetic and real samples share a close underlying distribution for CIFAR-10.

---

## Experiment: ResNet-18 Mixing

![](slide18_img1.png)

Trends mirror the CNN case: moderate synthetic ratios neither help nor harm significantly. Unlike CIFAR-100, realism gaps are small enough that mixing does not destabilize training.

---

## Experiment: Data Addition

![](slide19_img1.png)

Adding synthetic images (rather than replacing real ones) leads to clear gains:

- Accuracy grows as the dataset expands.  
- Performance peaks around **70% additional synthetic data** (≈1.7× dataset size).  
- Beyond this point, too much synthetic data reduces gains, likely due to distribution drift and redundancy.

---

## CIFAR-10 Conclusion

- For simple datasets with low intra-class variation, **synthetic images blend well** with real images.  
- When dataset size is fixed, synthetic replacement does not improve performance.  
- When the dataset is expanded, synthetic data provides meaningful gains—up to a threshold where domain shift begins to accumulate.

---

# Summary

Synthetic data can be either beneficial or harmful depending on dataset complexity, realism alignment, and how it is incorporated:

- **CIFAR-100 (complex dataset)**:  
  - Moderate synthetic ratios help weak classes but hurt strong ones.  
  - High synthetic ratios cause severe domain shift.  
  - Synthetic data is *not* a drop-in replacement for real samples.

- **CIFAR-10 (simple dataset)**:  
  - Synthetic and real data distributions are similar; mixing is stable.  
  - Performance improves mainly when synthetic data *increases total dataset size*.  
  - Gains peak before excessive synthetic content introduces drift.

Overall, synthetic data is most effective when used **as augmentation**, not substitution, and when tailored to dataset complexity. Future work could explore automated realism scoring, adaptive per-class generation, and domain-alignment techniques to further close the synthetic–real performance gap.
